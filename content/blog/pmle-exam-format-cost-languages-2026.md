## Summary

The standard Google Cloud Professional Machine Learning Engineer (PMLE) exam lasts **two hours**, contains **50–60 multiple-choice and multiple-select questions**, and costs **$200 plus tax where applicable**. Google lists **English and Japanese** as exam languages. You can take an online-proctored exam remotely or an onsite-proctored exam at a testing center.

Last checked October 16, 2026.

Testero is independent and not affiliated with Google.

## The standard exam at a glance

Google’s [official PMLE certification page](https://cloud.google.com/learn/certification/machine-learning-engineer) lists these details under “About this certification exam”:

- Length: two hours.
- Format: 50–60 multiple-choice and multiple-select questions.
- Registration fee: $200, plus tax where applicable.
- Languages: English and Japanese.
- Prerequisites: none.
- Recommended experience: three or more years of industry experience, including at least one year designing and managing solutions using Google Cloud.

These are standard exam details, not a separate renewal-exam offer. If you already hold the certification, follow Google’s linked renewal guidance before choosing what to book. This article does not infer a renewal price, duration, or eligibility window from the standard exam listing.

## What the two-hour format means for preparation

The question-count range matters: do not build your practice routine around the assumption that your exam will contain exactly 50 questions. Likewise, multiple-select questions deserve explicit practice alongside multiple-choice questions. Read the selection instructions rather than treating every item as a single-answer question.

For preparation, use a timed session to observe your own behavior. Note where you spend time rereading a scenario, which constraints you miss, and whether unfamiliar product names slow you down. The useful output is a list of problems to fix, not a practice percentage presented as an official readiness threshold.

Keep content coverage separate from pacing. The exam format tells you how questions are delivered; the exam guide tells you what to study. Our [PMLE exam domains and weights](/blog/pmle-exam-domains-weights) article helps you organize that second task. A comfortable pace cannot compensate for leaving a domain unreviewed.

## Budget for the listed fee, not an invented total

Google lists the registration fee as $200 with tax added where applicable. That is not a promise that every candidate’s final checkout total will be exactly $200. Check the amount presented during registration before paying, particularly if an employer is reimbursing the attempt.

A practical budget separates the exam registration from optional preparation spending. Do not assume a course, practice subscription, or lab budget is included in the registration price. Decide which preparation resources address a specific gap before buying more material.

This article does not estimate local taxes, currency conversions, voucher discounts, or retake costs. Those details need their own current checkout or policy evidence.

## Choose the exam language deliberately

The listed exam languages are English and Japanese. A translated website navigation menu is not evidence that the exam is available in every language shown on Google’s site.

Choose based on the language in which you can reason most accurately about technical constraints. As a study exercise, explain a deployment decision and its tradeoffs in that language without relying on memorized answer wording. Keep a small glossary for product names and terms that you recognize in documentation but hesitate over in questions.

Do not assume the language choice changes the knowledge you need. Use Google’s current exam guide as your content checklist and confirm the available language when registering.

## Remote or testing center: plan the environment

Google lists both online-proctored remote delivery and onsite-proctored testing-center delivery. Its certification page links to online testing requirements and a testing-center search. Read those linked requirements before committing to a delivery option; this overview is not a substitute for them.

For a remote appointment, make a checklist from the current provider requirements rather than guessing what equipment or room setup is acceptable. For a testing center, check the appointment location and travel plan before exam day. In either case, separate logistics from studying: resolve registration questions early enough that your last review session stays focused on technical gaps.
